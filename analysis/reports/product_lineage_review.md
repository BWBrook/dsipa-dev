# Product-lineage review for the 2013–2024 night-lights series

Date: 2026-09-17

## Decision

The retained Hengl/OpenGeoHub series crosses a documented upstream processing
boundary in 2022. Archived earlier-version code and collection labels support
the inference that the 2013–2021 inputs are EOG Annual VNL V2.1
`average_masked` layers, but they do not provide byte-level lineage for the
current v0.4 files. The retained 2022 raster identifies its source as the V2.2
combined SNPP/NOAA-20 `average_masked` composite. The retained 2023 and 2024
rasters identify SNPP-only EOG V2 `average_masked` inputs, although their
filenames do not say V2.1 or V2.2.

This boundary does not show that the 78 late-positive protected-area series are
artifacts. The completed audit found no discrete increase in first-detection
hazard in 2022, but the product lineage still prevents interpreting their
2022–2024 onset as environmental change. The defensible result is the observed
product-scale transition; its cause remains unidentified.

The v0.4 Zenodo description says that EOG Annual VNL V2 images for 2012–2024
were used and that original values on a 0–200 scale were converted to a
0–2000 scale. It does not give a year-by-year EOG version table. The current
script likewise does not preserve the `in.tifs` listing, and it contains no
step that builds the published 2012–2024 COGs from those inputs. Its positional
selection (`sel.tifs = in.tifs[c(3:13,1:2)]`) is therefore insufficient as a
lineage receipt without the original directory listing.

## What changed in 2022

The EOG readme gives three relevant V2.2 changes:

1. The 2022 composite uses 11 SNPP months, omitting August because of SNPP
   glitches, plus NOAA-20 data for May, August and September.
2. V2 and V2.1 excluded months with fewer than three cloud-free observations
   from the median. V2.2 includes months with more than one cloud-free
   observation.
3. In the data-range calculation, V2.1 included cells with no cloud-free
   observations as median zero; V2.2 excludes cells lacking median values.

These are changes in sensor composition and low-coverage/background handling.
They supply a credible measurement-process explanation for some first
detections in 2022. EOG still describes the annual output as a masked average
in nW cm^-2 sr^-1, but the masks on either side of 2022 are not generated by an
identical documented procedure. The retained source names also show that the
mixed SNPP/NOAA-20 composition is specific to 2022 in this series: the 2023 and
2024 source names revert to SNPP only. A transient 2022 pulse and a persistent
2022–2024 onset therefore have different diagnostic implications.

## Integer scaling and observed value quantum

The scale-metadata break is a storage-encoding change layered on top of the EOG
processing break:

- retained rasters have scale 1 through 2021 and scale 10 from 2022 onward;
- all retained rasters are integer encoded;
- metadata alone permits unit steps before 2022 and ten-unit steps afterward,
  but the permitted encoding is not the same as the values actually present.

In the audited Australian windows, every positive stored value in 2020 and 2021
was a multiple of 10 and the minimum was 10 (scale 1). From 2022 through 2024,
the minimum positive raw value was 1 (scale 10), so all decoded positives were
also multiples of 10 with minimum 10. There is therefore no evidence that the
observed decoded quantum became coarser at the 2022 boundary. The 2013 minimum
was also 10, but a greatest-common-divisor check returned 2 because of larger
values; it would be wrong to assert that all earlier raw positives lie on a
ten-unit grid. These checks describe stored values and do not identify EOG's
detection threshold.

The archived code does not fully explain the mixed encoding. The v0.2 script
shows one older conversion that writes V2.1 inputs to integer and assigns scale
10; the current v0.4 files instead use scale 1 for those years. The v0.4 script
omits the final COG conversion. Consequently, the statement that both blocks
represent the intended 0–2000 product scale is supported by the Zenodo
description and raster metadata, but the exact transformation is not
reproducible from the deposited code. Raw-value inspection should remain in
the audit receipt.

## Lowest-cost external anchors

### 1. Native EOG layers: first choice for identity and encoding checks

EOG V2.1 and V2.2 downloads are behind the EOG OpenID login. A free EOG account
is the only prerequisite. Downloading or extracting the native
`average_masked` layers for 2021 and 2022 is the lowest-cost way to test whether
OpenGeoHub changed pixel values, masks, alignment or scaling. It does not
provide an independent environmental test because the EOG series itself
crosses the documented V2.1/V2.2 boundary.

Google Earth Engine also hosts the native collections as
[`NOAA/VIIRS/DNB/ANNUAL_V21`](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NOAA_VIIRS_DNB_ANNUAL_V21)
and
[`NOAA/VIIRS/DNB/ANNUAL_V22`](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NOAA_VIIRS_DNB_ANNUAL_V22).
If an authenticated research Earth Engine project is already available, a
targeted extraction for the 78 reserves and controls is cheaper than global
downloads. It remains the same EOG source, not an independent anchor.

Live access probe on 2026-09-17: both EOG annual directories returned HTTP 302
to the EOG OpenID service. No EOG token/user environment entry, Earth Engine
CLI/configuration, Earthdata token environment entry, or `.netrc` was present
in this WSL environment. Thus an authenticated native extraction cannot be run
non-interactively from the current session without the user completing a login
or supplying an already-created token through the normal credential store.

### 2. NASA Black Marble: independent sensitivity anchor if the EOG check is ambiguous

NASA's
[`VNP46A4.002`](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/VNP46A4)
is a yearly 15-arc-second SNPP VIIRS product beginning in 2012. Collection 2
applies moonlight and atmospheric corrections and year-specific SNPP spectral
response functions. It is methodologically distinct from EOG VNL and is
therefore useful as an independent directional check, not as a drop-in
replacement.

Access requires a free NASA Earthdata Login and an Earthdata token for scripted
LAADS downloads. The economical design is to obtain only 2021–2023 tiles that
intersect the late-positive reserves and matched near-lit/far-from-lit controls,
and to extract the snow-free annual composite plus observation/quality layers.
A national 2013–2024 re-analysis would be disproportionate at this stage.
The LAADS archive index was publicly reachable in the access probe, but data
downloads require authentication; no local Earthdata credential was found.

## Audit result and interpretation

The complete-series inside-reserve first-detection hazard was 14/3,864
(0.362%) in 2022, compared with 13/3,877 (0.335%) in 2021. In the stratum more
than 20 km from a positive 2013 pixel centre, the corresponding counts were
2/1,239 in 2022 and 3/1,242 in 2021. The higher hazards occurred in 2023 and
2024, not at the documented 2022 product boundary. The audit therefore found no
discrete national 2022 excess consistent with a simple version-switch artifact.

Distance strata are defined against the fixed 2013 product state. They avoid
using later outcomes to define exposure, but proximity remains an association,
not identification: it cannot distinguish lighting encroachment from correlated
development, spatial processing behaviour or other causes. Persistence also
does not identify cause. The 78 cases remain product-scale monitoring flags,
not evidence of newly established lighting.

Because the manuscript retains that descriptive interpretation and the audit
found no 2022 discontinuity, an external-product anchor is not required before
the next reading bundle. Black Marble becomes necessary only if a stronger
environmental-onset claim is contemplated or a later diagnostic contradicts
the current result.

## Remaining limitations

- The retained tags establish SNPP-only 2023 and 2024 inputs, but their generic
  `v2` filenames do not establish which V2.x mask algorithm EOG used.
- EOG's public documentation specifies the 2022 V2.2 changes but does not state
  that later annual releases are algorithmically identical.
- The OpenGeoHub deposit does not archive a complete, executable construction
  path for the published 2012–2024 COGs, so the mixed scale metadata cannot be
  explained solely from deposited code.
- Black Marble agreement would be corroboration across processing chains, not
  proof that a protected-area change was caused by ALAN encroachment.
