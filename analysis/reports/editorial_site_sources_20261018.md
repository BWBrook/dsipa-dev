# Candidate site context: primary-source verification, 18 October 2026

These sources were checked for Claude's exploratory additive-budget candidate.
They are not incorporated in the round-1 manuscript. The coordinates below are
candidate night-light cluster centroids reported by Claude, not independently
recalculated here. Sources establish site geography and chronology, not lamp
identity, source attribution, ecological consequences or protection effectiveness.

## Kosciuszko: Lobs Hole and Tantangara

Snowy Hydro's EPL 21266 monitoring report for June–November 2024, Table 1-1,
printed pp.7–8, identifies:

- EPL5, 35.785°S 148.416°E, upstream of the exploratory tunnel/construction pad
  at Lobs Hole: approximately 0.29 km from the supplied western cluster centre
  (35.783°S 148.414°E). EPL4/25, 35.788°S 148.415°E, identify portal access.
- EPL32, 35.790°S 148.659°E, Tantangara Intake downstream of construction works:
  approximately 0.21 km from the supplied eastern centre (35.791°S 148.657°E).

These are environmental monitoring points, not lamp positions. Official report:
https://www.snowyhydro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/S2-FGJV-ENV-REP-0116A.pdf

Figure 1-4, p.15 of the official Visual Impact Management Plan maps the Lobs Hole
and Tantangara construction envelopes and infrastructure. The map was visually
checked but not georeferenced against the candidate raster pixels:
https://www.snowyhydro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Snowy-2.0-Main-Works-Visual-Impact-Management-Plan-Stage-1-Rev-K.pdf

The August 2020 project booklet, printed p.8 / PDF p.5, states that exploratory
works had been underway at Lobs Hole since 2019 and describes 2020 earthworks,
roads, pads and bridges:
https://www.snowyhydro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Snowy-2.0-booklet_August-2020.pdf

The Summer 2020 newsletter (issue 51), p.4, reports Tantangara work newly underway
after October approvals, including clearing, road preparation and bulk earthworks.
It also describes continuous construction shifts at Lobs Hole. Thus a common
2019 construction start should not be assigned to both sites:
https://www.snowyhydro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/SH_53306_Snowy_Hydro_Community_Newsletter_Nov2020_WEB-1.pdf

## Barrow Island and Gorgon

The WA Government's 21 March 2016 release identifies Barrow Island as a Class A
Nature Reserve hosting Gorgon, records the first LNG shipment and dates the start
of construction to December 2009. It lists the plant, jetty, accommodation and
related infrastructure:
https://www.wa.gov.au/government/media-statements/Barnett%20Liberal%20National%20Government/Premier-hails-first-Gorgon-LNG-shipment-20160321

Chevron announced LNG and condensate production on 7 March 2016:
https://chevroncorp.gcs-web.com/node/13991

Chevron Australia records first cargo departure on 21 March 2016:
https://australia.chevron.com/what-we-do/gorgon-project

Keep first production distinct from first cargo, and retain that construction
predated the satellite series. A 2016 radiance rise is not automatically lighting
onset. Subject to independent trajectory checks, the most these sources warrant
is spatial and temporal consistency with documented site activity.
