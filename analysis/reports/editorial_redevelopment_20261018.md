Baseline: main manuscript at commit 59725d3, including Claude's 28 September

## Editorial diagnosis

The abstract gives nearly equal weight to every result. The introduction ends
with a modest description of a national application, while the discussion
repeats the safeguards surrounding it. Readers are asked to infer the unifying
contribution. Better sentence flow alone has not solved that structural problem.

Proposed question: **What does an apparently unchanged protected area actually
tell a satellite monitoring programme?**

Proposed argument: distinguish unassessable reserves, unchanged recorded series,
zero full-period slopes with changing annual observations, and change in the
surrounding landscape. Quantify these distinctions using the Australian estate,
then show the different investigations they motivate. A competing structure
leads with inside/surrounding change and uses the temporal analysis to explain
why monitoring also needs annual observations. Claude has been asked to argue
against either framing if it fails the desk-editor or specialist test.

3. **Beyond reserve-wide trends (implemented).** Lead with 249 surrounding-only
   positive cases and the selected-cohort boundary comparison. Explain what a

   empirical Australian monitoring contribution, without claiming that paired
   comparisons or reporting distinctions are methodologically new.

not adopted: positive slopes, negative slopes and annual variation are not

### Literature check and claim boundaries

A separate read-only check used original papers/publisher records. It rejects
“prior assessments usually report one percentage” and “choices rarely made
explicit”. The first-page comparison now recognises established precedent.

- Gaston, Duffy & Bennie (2015), doi:10.1111/cobi.12462, explicitly distinguish
  protected-area counts from area weighting and discuss large reserves; they
  also report brightness classes and temporal trends. Full text:
  https://kevingaston.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/Gaston_et_al-2015-quantifying-erosion-of-natural-darkness-in-GPA.pdf
- Guetté et al. (2018), doi:10.1016/j.biocon.2018.04.018, compare interiors with
  outer belts below 25 km and at 25–75 km. The accessible publisher abstract and
  introduction establish surrounding change despite relatively stable interiors;
  detailed methods were not available in that check:
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006320717319043
- Mu et al. (2021), doi:10.3390/rs13091849, distinguish continuous/discontinuous
  lighting histories and spatial distance zones, reporting counts and areas.
  This is precedent for temporal and spatial differentiation. Their headline
  denominators appear inconsistent across abstract and Results; no such totals
  are reproduced in our draft. Publisher: https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/9/1849
- Koen et al. (2018), doi:10.1111/gcb.14146, concerns light extent and species
  richness rather than a protected-area monitoring framework. Publisher abstract
  checked; no new citation was needed for the narrower current argument:
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/gcb.14146

The optional policy anchor was checked separately: Australia's May 2023 National
Light Pollution Guidelines use a nominal precautionary 20 km distance when
considering species-specific assessment for projects near important habitat of
listed light-sensitive species (printed p.10). This is not a general protected-area
buffer or physical boundary of impacts. It was not added to this draft: the
1 km comparison has a different purpose, and the management discussion already
specifies the observations needed for site follow-up. Official source:
https://www.dcceew.gov.au/system/files/resources/2eb379de-931b-4547-8bcc-f96c73065f54/files/national-light-pollution-guidelines-wildlife.pdf
