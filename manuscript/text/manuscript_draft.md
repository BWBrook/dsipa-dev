# Boundary zones and interior hotspots of brightening in Australia's protected areas

## Abstract

Satellite night-lights can reveal change across a protected-area estate, but reserve-wide averages obscure where change occurs and which places drive the national total. Using annual VIIRS-derived observations for 2013–2024, we estimated trends in area-integrated radiance separately for the outermost kilometre and deeper interior of each reserve in Australia's 2024 protected estate. Of 11,133 reserves, 4,426 (39.8%) had sufficient raster support, containing 99.8% of summed reserve area. The outermost kilometre held 14% of sampled area but contributed 72% of the aggregate increase, calculated by summing positive slopes across both zones; its share was 83% for 2013–2021. Even among reserves with measurable deeper interiors, half the increase lay in the outer kilometre, occupying one-eighth of sampled area. Increases farther inward were concentrated: two reserves supplied more than half and seven supplied 80%. In Kosciuszko National Park, the largest interior contributor, two clusters near documented Snowy 2.0 construction areas accounted for 96% of positive pixel changes across the whole park between 2019 and 2024. Decreases were more concentrated still: five reserves supplied 95%, including 90% from Barrow Island and Murujuga alone. These few reserves determined the sign of the national balance: the net trend reversed between the full period and 2013–2021, but was positive in both periods when they were excluded. Twelve of the twenty largest integrated contributors ranked outside the top hundred by mean trend. Shared first-detection increases in 2017 and 2023 left processing effects unresolved; all results describe a masked, quantised satellite product rather than ground illumination or biological effects. Separating boundary, interior and annual records reveals changes that a national net trend conceals and directs investigation towards specific sites and their lighting histories.

## Keywords

artificial light at night; protected areas; VIIRS; remote sensing; conservation monitoring

## 1. Introduction

A small, brightly lit site and a weak increase across many kilometres can produce the same average change in a large protected area. They imply different investigations: one concerns a local source, the other a more dispersed pressure. Reserve-wide satellite trends alone cannot distinguish them. For agencies monitoring entire protected-area estates, the task is therefore to locate change and establish what contributes to it, as well as to count reserves that are brightening.

Artificial light alters wildlife behaviour, physiology and species interactions, and scattered light can brighten the sky far beyond its source [@longcore2004; @gaston2013; @hirt2023]. Satellite assessments have documented lighting within and around protected areas using reserve counts, area summaries and surrounding-land comparisons [@gaston2015; @guette2018; @mu2021]. Skyglow also reaches conservation-priority areas [@garrett2020]. These studies establish the reach of the pressure. A complementary question is how the measured increase is distributed: between boundary zones and deeper interiors, and among the reserves making up a national estate.

Two distinctions matter. First, assigning a reserve to a positive-trend class counts its entire polygon area, even if change is confined to a small part. Summing change over observed pixels instead measures the contribution of that reserve to an aggregate product signal. Second, averaging over a large reserve can dilute a concentrated increase. A site ranking based on mean trends can consequently differ from one based on area-integrated change. Neither ranking alone identifies ecological impact, but together they distinguish different scales of change for follow-up.

Annual night-lights permit these comparisons across large estates. Since 2012, the day/night band (DNB) of the Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) has recorded upward radiance with a ground footprint of about 750 m. Annual composites combine cloud-free observations and remove dim background noise and transient sources such as fires [@elvidge2021]. Small or narrow reserves may contain too few pixels for assessment, and low-radiance observations may be recorded as zero. Changes in product construction complicate temporal interpretation, while a median trend through a mostly zero series can remain zero despite recent positive observations. Annual records are therefore needed alongside spatial summaries. The analysis concerns satellite upward radiance; translating it into sky brightness, ground illumination or biological effects requires further observations [@falchi2016; @kyba2023; @levin2025; @bara2025].

We used Australia's 2024 protected-area estate to ask where measured radiance increased and decreased during 2013–2024, and how concentrated those changes were. We compared reserves with surrounding unprotected land, divided reserve totals into boundary and interior components, and examined locations behind the largest contributions. Most positive change lay near boundaries; much of the remainder was concentrated in a few reserves. Large decreases at still fewer sites determined whether the national balance was positive or negative. Annual histories and local raster comparisons reveal the different changes behind those totals.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Estate, annual observations and measurement limits

We used the 2024 Collaborative Australian Protected Areas Database (CAPAD), dissolving polygons by stable protected-area identifier (`PA_ID`) and repairing invalid geometries. The 11,133 resulting identifiers were the analytical units. Distance and area calculations used the Australian equal-area projection EPSG:3577. Applying this fixed estate retrospectively makes the analysis a history of land protected in 2024; it does not estimate the effect of designation or follow annual changes in reserve boundaries.

Annual inputs were the openly distributed 2013–2024 VIIRS-derived Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs in the Hengl/OpenGeoHub night-lights dataset, version 0.4 [@hengl2025nightlights], which follows the Annual VIIRS Nighttime Lights (VNL) V2 methodology [@elvidge2021]. We used these annual layers and excluded the dataset's earlier extrapolated years. Each file's scale and offset were applied, including the scale-metadata change in 2022–2024. Retained source tags identify the 2022 input as a V2.2 masked composite combining Suomi-NPP and NOAA-20 observations, and the 2023–2024 inputs as Suomi-NPP masked composites. An earlier archived script supports V2.1 masked inputs for 2013–2021 but does not fully reproduce the later deposit's encoding (Supporting Methods S2). We call the metadata-adjusted values *product-scaled radiance* and report them in scaled product units, without conversion to physical radiance, ground illuminance or sky brightness.

In every year, the smallest positive decoded pixel value in the Australian audit window was 10 product units. Spatial means can be smaller because they average positive and zero pixels. Integer quantisation and upstream masking both constrain low-radiance observations; their separate contributions remain unresolved. Zeros were retained as product non-detections, negative codes and no-data values were excluded, and missing measurements remained missing. Annual layers were neither smoothed nor interpolated. A continuity audit examined stored values, metadata-adjusted distributions and the timing of first-positive observations across the 2022 source boundary.

### 2.2 Spatial comparisons and eligibility

For each reserve and year, we calculated mean radiance, median, 95th percentile and the fraction of valid pixels greater than zero. Extraction included a cell only when its centre fell within the polygon (`all_touched=False`), and selected valid cells were weighted equally. Mean radiance was the primary summary; the other metrics described the distribution and its zero mass.

The primary surrounding comparison used a 1 km outward buffer with ocean and all protected tenure removed, termed the *unprotected-land buffer*. A 5 km buffer supplied a wider comparison. We also calculated raw geometric and land-masked buffers to distinguish the effects of excluding ocean and neighbouring protected land. Land masking used Australian Coastline 50K 2024 version 1-1, derived from 2022–2024 Sentinel-2 imagery and approximating mean high water [@hammerton2024coastline]. Six reserve polygons with no intersection with this land source were excluded from land-dependent comparisons; none belonged to the eligible inside cohort. Comparisons near documented coastline-source exceptions were flagged as provisional for local map validation (Supporting Methods S3).

Buffers and inward bands used repaired, unsimplified CAPAD boundaries and projected EPSG:3577 distances. Circular arcs used 128 segments per quadrant, giving a maximum chord error below 0.19 m at 10 km. The extraction required at least 10 valid pixels per zone-year at the product's nominal 500 m resolution, and slopes required at least eight valid years. These rules were applied separately to each zone, with failed summaries treated as missing. Eligibility therefore depended on geometry and available observations. Pixel counts measure raster support, not independent ecological samples.

Interior bands extended 0–1, 1–5 and 5–10 km inward. We described 2024 patterns for reserves eligible in all three bands, then compared temporal slopes for the whole reserve and the 1–5 km band among reserves eligible for both and for the 1 km surrounding comparison. The surrounding slope was held fixed when substituting the deeper-band slope for the whole-reserve slope. A subsequent review-stage check added 0–1 km slopes and annual zero status in this same matched cohort. Whole-reserve and band slopes were compared by sign; they are not additive components of a whole-reserve slope.

### 2.3 Trends and annual trajectories

Theil–Sen slopes summarised 2013–2024 change in annual mean product-scaled radiance. The estimator is the median slope between all pairs of valid years, limiting the influence of isolated anomalous observations. Paired inside and surrounding slopes defined four descriptive categories: both positive (Q1), only inside positive (Q2), neither positive (Q3), and only surrounding positive (Q4). Non-positive includes zero and negative slopes. Categories used strict point-estimate signs without a significance or minimum-effect threshold; the inside-minus-surrounding slope was retained as a descriptive contrast.

National summaries counted reserve identifiers and summed the full polygon areas assigned to each category. These area totals can count overlapping designations more than once and do not estimate the land area illuminated or undergoing change. To examine the magnitudes behind the positive class's area share, we divided its 420 slopes into three equally sized groups by rank and summed their polygon areas. This descriptive tercile analysis was added during manuscript review; its divisions carry no ecological-effect interpretation.

For every exactly zero inside slope, we inspected the annual means and counted all-zero series, constant non-zero series and series with any variation. A separate late-positive pattern required all 12 annual values to be valid, all nine values in 2013–2021 to be zero, and at least one positive value in 2022–2024. This review-stage check retained the original trend categories. To examine product compatibility, annual first-positive fractions were calculated among complete, previously all-zero series for reserves and both surrounding buffers. Inside fractions were stratified by fixed distance from positive 2013 raster-cell centres, avoiding a reference mask informed by later detections (Supporting Methods S2). These diagnostics describe the timing of product observations, without identifying its cause.

Four reserves illustrated contrasting annual trajectories: Neerabup and Henderson in Western Australia, Black Mountain in the Australian Capital Territory, and Bungonia in New South Wales. Black Mountain was selected specifically to show dimming within Q3. The sites are illustrative, not representative of their categories or ranked management priorities.

### 2.4 Additive change budget and site inspection

To quantify contributions to aggregate change, we multiplied each annual reserve mean by its valid-pixel count and an approximate cell area, then fitted an ordinary least-squares (OLS) slope to the resulting annual totals. Cell area was calculated for the 15 arc-second grid at each reserve's centroid latitude and held constant through time. This yields area-integrated product radiance over annual valid support, in product units × km²; it is neither emitted light nor a deduplicated land integral. Overlapping designations contribute separately. All 4,426 eligible reserves and their outermost kilometre had complete 12-year summaries. Subtracting the outer-band total from the whole-reserve total gave the remainder beyond 1 km, including any interior beyond 5 km. OLS slopes are additive across these components, whereas median pairwise slopes are not.

We defined gross positive change as the sum of positive slopes, retaining negative slopes separately. At reserve level, this describes concentration among reserves. For the boundary/interior split, the denominator instead sums positive slopes over both bands in every reserve. It can exceed reserve-level gross change because opposite signs within a reserve cancel in its whole-reserve total. These descriptive budgets use no significance threshold and do not sum positive changes pixel by pixel. We repeated the split for 2013–2021 and for the difference between mean totals in 2022–2024 and 2013–2015. Additional checks excluded four reserves with changing valid-pixel counts and compared reserve ranks using the same OLS estimator for mean and integrated radiance (Supporting Methods S4). To identify the influence of large decreases, we also recomputed national net slopes after excluding the same five reserves selected by their largest negative 2013–2024 contributions. This post-inspection diagnostic quantifies their influence; it does not redefine the estate or estimate a national trend with those observations removed.

Kosciuszko was selected after the budget identified it as the largest positive contributor. We inspected the source raster cells for 2019 and 2024, grouping adjacent cells with positive differences into clusters and comparing their locations with official Snowy 2.0 documents. This endpoint comparison uses positive pixel differences, a third denominator distinct from positive reserve or band slopes. It locates measured change without attributing individual pixels to particular lights or establishing environmental onset. We also inspected Barrow Island, Murujuga and Thevenard Island, selected during review from the largest negative contributors, and compared their histories and local changes with documented industrial sites (Supporting Methods S5). Their endpoint comparisons locate change in selected years, rather than decomposing the full-period OLS slopes.

### 2.5 Temporal sensitivity and reproducibility

Archived moving-block resampling summaries describe the temporal sensitivity of selected slopes. Blocks of three consecutive year-value pairs were sampled separately for each reserve, and recomputed slopes were pooled by reserve. The original procedure sampled Interim Biogeographic Regionalisation for Australia (IBRA) regions with replacement in 300 outer iterations; a reserve was resampled whenever its dominant region was drawn. Draw counts therefore vary among reserves. Marginal medians, 2.5th–97.5th percentiles and fractions of positive resampled slopes are sensitivity diagnostics, with neither calibrated confidence coverage nor joint spatial uncertainty (Supporting Methods S1).

The Python pipeline records source versions, checksums, pinned dependencies, stable output keys and run manifests. Surrounding and interior observations were re-extracted after independent review identified coarse buffer geometry and an unsuitable land mask. Exact keyed checks verified unchanged whole-reserve observations and slopes. Supporting Methods S3 reports spatial-support convergence and the separate geometry and coastline comparisons. Corrected outputs retain their own manifest alongside the earlier archived baseline.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 National coverage and reserve-wide trends

The satellite product supported assessment of 4,426 of the 11,133 reserves (39.8%), containing 99.8% of summed polygon area (Table 1; Fig. S1). The 6,707 excluded identifiers accounted for the remaining 0.2%. Paired inside and 1 km surrounding trends were available for 4,253 reserves, and the 5 km comparison for 4,370. These denominators reflect the geometry and raster support of each comparison.

Among the 4,426 eligible reserves, 3,844 (86.9%) had zero inside mean radiance in 2024. The 582 with positive means were concentrated near developed coastal and urban regions. Whole-reserve slopes across 2013–2024 were positive for 420 reserves (9.5%), negative for 37 (0.8%) and exactly zero for 3,969 (89.7%). Thus an inside-positive trend screen selected fewer than one in ten assessable reserves. The additive budget, using OLS rather than median pairwise slopes, identified 560 positive and 75 negative whole-reserve contributions (Fig. 1).

### 3.2 Boundary zones dominate the positive change budget

The outermost kilometre accounted for 72.0% of the gross positive band-level trend, although it contained only 13.7% of sampled valid area (Fig. 2). The share was 82.5% in 2013–2021 and 71.5% using the late-minus-early period difference. Restricting the comparison to 1,398 reserves with at least ten valid pixels beyond 1 km in every year reduced the boundary share to 51.2%, in 12.5% of their sampled area. Small reserves lying entirely or largely within the outer kilometre thus contributed to the national concentration. Excluding the four changing-count reserves gave 72.0% nationally and 51.2% in the supported-interior subset (Table S6). These are spatial allocations of the recorded signal; they do not identify the lighting source.

Surrounding-land comparisons showed why the boundary also matters for reserves without positive inside trends. Of 596 reserves with positive surrounding Sen slopes, 249 had non-positive inside slopes (Fig. 3). The 4,253 paired reserves comprised 347 with both slopes positive (Q1; 8.2%), 70 with only inside positive (Q2; 1.6%), 3,587 with neither positive (Q3; 84.3%) and 249 with only surrounding positive (Q4; 5.9%).

The separate matched Sen-slope comparison showed a similar spatial pattern: of 150 positive whole-reserve slopes, 100 had positive outer-band but zero 1–5 km slopes, and 85 of those also had positive surrounding slopes (Tables S3–S4). Only 369 reserves supported the complete three-band profile (Fig. S2); these geometry-selected comparisons use different supports and estimators from the additive budget.

### 3.3 Concentrated interior increases and decreases

The remainder beyond 1 km supplied 28.0% of gross positive band-level change. Two reserves accounted for more than half of this interior contribution and seven for 80%. Kosciuszko National Park alone supplied 47.1%. Across the whole reserve, its unweighted sum of decoded pixel values increased from 1,220 in 2019 to 26,970 in 2024 (Fig. 4). Two clusters accounted for 25,180 of 26,110 summed positive pixel differences (96.4%); other cells decreased by 360. Both clusters had zero product values in 2019. Kosciuszko ranked 286th by mean OLS slope but first by integrated OLS slope. Twelve of the twenty largest integrated contributors ranked outside the mean-slope top 100 (Fig. 2).

The cluster centres, near 35.783°S, 148.414°E and 35.791°S, 148.657°E, lie close to the documented Lobs Hole and Tantangara construction areas of Snowy 2.0 [@snowymonitor2025]. Lobs Hole exploratory works were underway from 2019; Tantangara works were documented as beginning after approvals in October 2020 [@snowybooklet2020; @snowysummer2020]. The whole-park cell-sum had already risen from 1,150 in 2020 to 4,600 in 2021, before the documented 2022 source change, and increased in every subsequent year. Timing and proximity make construction lighting a plausible explanation for investigation. The product non-detections in 2019 do not establish an absence of lighting.

Across whole reserves, gross positive OLS slopes summed to 2,709 product units × km² per year and gross negative slopes to −2,231, leaving a net of +479. Thirty reserves supplied half of the positive contribution and 115 supplied 80%; Kosciuszko supplied 13.6%. Five reserves supplied 94.9% of the gross decrease, led by Barrow Island (55.6%) and Murujuga (34.4%). The national net was −538 for 2013–2021. Excluding the same five largest full-period negative contributors gave positive net slopes in both periods: +2,596 for 2013–2024 and +1,922 for 2013–2021 (Table S7). A handful of reserves therefore determined the sign of the estate-wide balance.

Three named decreases had industrial context (Fig. S7). Barrow Island's cell-sum nearly tripled from 74,690 in 2015 to 207,180 in 2016, with 99.9% of positive pixel differences in one cluster near the Gorgon plant; LNG production was announced in March 2016 [@chevrongorgon2015; @chevron2016]. Later values declined with intervening rebounds. Murujuga's sum fell by 86.2% between 2013 and 2024, with 96.2% of negative pixel differences concentrated near the Karratha Gas Plant [@dcceewkgp2025]. Thevenard's sum fell from 3,370 to zero over the same endpoints, near its former oil facility. Production ceased in 2014 and onshore decommissioning was completed by June 2023 [@chevronthevenard2014; @chevron2023]. Its zero observations in 2016–2017 were followed by positive values in 2018–2019, before zero values in 2020–2024. These site histories identify plausible operational explanations; they do not establish the causes of the satellite changes.

Classifying whole polygons by their Sen-slope signs assigned 37.6% of eligible area to positive trends, although the lowest third of positive slopes accounted for 99.1% of that class's area (Table S4). Such polygon shares do not estimate the area that brightened; the additive budget instead measures contributions over sampled pixels.

### 3.4 Annual changes concealed by zero slopes

Every zero-slope reserve had a complete 12-year record. Most (3,786) were zero throughout and three had constant non-zero means, but 180 (4.5%) varied between years (Fig. S5). Of these, 78 (2.0% of zero-slope reserves) had nine zero years followed by at least one positive observation in 2022–2024.

The timing of first detections raised a product-compatibility concern. First-positive fractions rose synchronously in 2017 and 2023 inside reserves and in both surrounding cohorts (Fig. S4; Table S2). Inside first detections numbered 53 in 2017, 14 in 2022, 31 in 2023 and 33 in 2024, placing 64 of the 78 late-positive histories in 2023–2024. Among audit-window pixels valid throughout 2020–2024, zero-to-positive transitions increased from 28,035 in 2022 to 57,328 in 2023, while positive-to-zero transitions fell from 21,520 to 12,031. The absence of a spike in 2022 itself did not resolve processing effects on subsequent detections.

Black Mountain illustrates variation hidden within the non-positive category (Fig. S6). Its inside and surrounding slopes were −1.037 and −3.952 product units per year, placing a dimming record in the same Q3 category as an all-zero record. The other trajectories in Fig. S6 show contrasting inside and surrounding histories.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Two spatial patterns behind the national trend

Measured increases were concentrated near reserve boundaries and, farther inward, in a few large contributors. The outer kilometre held about one-seventh of sampled area but nearly three-quarters of positive band-level change. Beyond it, two reserves supplied more than half the remaining increase. Kosciuszko illustrates the second pattern: two localised clusters near documented construction areas produced a substantial reserve total. These results give a national satellite screen a more specific purpose than assigning reserves to trend classes: identifying the locations and spatial scales that require investigation.

The boundary concentration persisted under a shorter period and a different temporal summary, although its magnitude changed. Reserve geometry contributed to the national result. Even among reserves with supported deeper interiors, however, half the increase lay in the outer kilometre, occupying only one-eighth of their sampled area. The national result describes where the observed increase lies across this estate, not a common edge effect of equal strength within every reserve. The net national direction depended on a few large decreases: removing the same five influential reserves left a positive balance in both periods. Their exclusion diagnoses concentration, rather than justifying their removal from national monitoring. A single net trend would conceal both sets of locations. Reporting positive and negative contributions separately preserves changes of potential management interest even when they cancel nationally.

Mean radiance and area-integrated radiance also answer different questions. The first describes average change over a reserve's sampled support; the second describes its contribution to a summed product signal. A large reserve can have a small mean slope yet contribute strongly through a localised increase. Conversely, a small reserve with a steep mean slope need not dominate aggregate change. Neither quantity measures ecological consequence. The relevant scale depends on whether an agency is investigating conditions within a reserve, contributions to regional lighting, or particular sensitive habitats.

Earlier assessments established the value of reserve-level, area-weighted and surrounding-land comparisons [@gaston2015; @guette2018; @mu2021]. This analysis adds an explicit account of where the summed measured change occurs and connects it to inspectable site histories. It does not imply that all interior change represents encroachment: protected areas include inhabited and managed landscapes, with facilities and permitted land uses. Site interpretation requires the relevant custodians' knowledge, land-use history and conservation objectives.

### 4.2 What boundary and interior patterns can establish

A spatial allocation of satellite radiance does not identify its source. Of the 100 matched outer-positive, deeper-zero cases, 85 also had brightening surroundings. Local lighting near a boundary and spatial mixing of nearby sources by the sensor can both produce this pattern. The approximately 750 m DNB footprint, resampled onto a nominal 500 m grid, is large relative to the outer band. Product masking can also remove faint deeper observations. The 15 cases without positive surrounding slopes show that the outer-band pattern is not always accompanied by the same sign outside, but do not identify an internal source.

The Kosciuszko clusters provide more precise targets for investigation because their locations can be compared with documented infrastructure. Their correspondence with construction areas supports a testable explanation, rather than demonstrating the cause or environmental consequences of the signal. A rise was already evident in 2021, before the documented 2022 source change. That change alone cannot explain the earlier observation, although timing and spatial concentration do not exclude other processing effects. Independently processed observations, lighting inventories, construction records and ground measurements would help distinguish source changes from measurement effects.

These patterns suggest different routes for management. Boundary flags can be overlaid with nearby developments and lighting inventories to identify sources and examine land-use approvals on either side of the reserve boundary. Australia's national guidelines recommend species-specific assessment for projects within a nominal 20 km of important habitat for listed species known to be affected by artificial light [@dcceew2023]; our 1 km sampling band is not an impact boundary. For concentrated site signals, annual records can be compared with construction, operation and decommissioning histories, then used to target checks of lighting design and approval conditions. Flaring or other operational changes require separate interpretation from ordinary lighting. Sky-brightness and ground-illumination measurements, together with habitat and species information, establish which satellite signals correspond to conservation concerns.

### 4.3 Annual records belong beside trend categories

Full-period slopes and annual observations answer different temporal questions. With nine zero years in a 12-year series, 36 of the 66 pairwise slopes are zero, enough to fix the median at zero even if all three later values increase. The 180 varying zero-slope records demonstrate the practical consequence. Their categories correctly describe the full-period median slope while omitting variation that a monitoring programme may want to examine.

The 78 late-positive records require a further distinction between a product detection and environmental onset. First-detection increases shared by inside and surrounding cohorts in 2017 and 2023 leave product processing entangled with calendar time. The mixed-sensor 2022 composite could have suppressed faint detections before the 2023 increase, but the diagnostics do not establish that explanation. Persistence in later years cannot resolve it either. A recent-detection flag therefore needs product-compatibility checks before environmental interpretation. Independently processed annual observations and ground measurements would help test these cases.

For routine reporting, each reserve's trend should remain linked to its annual values, eligibility and spatial comparison. The national account can then show how many reserves are assessable, which zones have positive or negative slopes, how positive and negative contributions are distributed, and how magnitude and polygon area shape the summary. This avoids treating a dimming site such as Black Mountain as equivalent to an all-zero site merely because both occupy Q3. It also gives managers an inspectable route from a national summary to the observations behind an individual flag.

### 4.4 Measurement limits and future use

The selected product measures upward radiance in the sensor's spectral range. Changing lighting technologies, atmospheric propagation and spectral sensitivity separate this quantity from ground illumination and sky brightness [@falchi2016; @kyba2023; @levin2025; @bara2025]. White light-emitting diode (LED) conversions can reduce DNB radiance without reducing visible illumination because the sensor is insensitive below approximately 500 nm [@kyba2017]. Black Mountain's declining product radiance consequently establishes neither ecological darkening nor an LED conversion. At low radiance, the observed ten-unit pixel floor and unresolved contributions of masking and quantisation prevent interpreting the 86.9% zero share as a measure of ecological darkness. Applying scale metadata reconciles the stored encodings but cannot remove these observational constraints.

Coverage is selective even though eligible reserves hold almost all summed polygon area. Small and narrow reserves are under-represented among the 39.8% assessed, and their monitoring needs remain. The matched Sen-slope comparison concerns 1,341 reserves, while only 369 supported the complete three-band profile; neither subset establishes a national rate of light penetration. The additive budget covers all 4,426 assessable reserves but uses centroid-based cell areas and annually observed support. Stable counts do not prove identical valid pixels. Its designation-level totals retain overlapping reserves and should not be read as a unique-land integral. Nine paired comparisons near documented coastline exceptions remain provisional pending local map checks (Supporting Methods S3). Marginal resampling ranges also omit measurement error, boundary uncertainty and spatial covariance, so they cannot establish joint category confidence or an ordering of reserves.

Future assessments should preserve both boundary and product identities, separating changes in the monitoring estate from changes in observations. The fixed 2024 estate used here includes land designated during the study period and supports no inference about protection's effectiveness. Biological consequences require their own evidence. Repeated surveys with detection, effort and habitat information would support that next step more directly than opportunistic occurrence records, whose sampling processes can confound ecological interpretation [@phillips2009; @fithian2015; @baker2024].

Across the assessable estate, most positive change occurred near reserve boundaries, while a few reserves supplied much of the deeper increase and almost all of the decrease. Their opposing contributions could reverse the national net trend. Monitoring that retains these locations and their annual histories can distinguish boundary change from concentrated site activity and connect each to the relevant source checks, lighting decisions and ecological observations.
