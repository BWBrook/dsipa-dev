# Satellite screening of lighting change across Australia's protected areas

## Abstract

Annual satellite night-lights offer a repeatable way to screen protected areas for changing lighting conditions, but low radiance, small reserves and complex boundaries constrain their conservation use. We assessed 2013-2024 VIIRS-derived annual radiance across Australia's 2024 protected-area estate, comparing reserve interiors with surrounding unprotected land and examining interior bands where raster support permitted. Of 11,133 protected areas, 4,426 (39.8%) met the pixel and temporal eligibility rules, representing 99.8% of summed reserve area. Among these, 3,844 (86.9%) had zero inside mean radiance in 2024 and 420 (9.5%) had positive full-period Theil-Sen slopes. A zero slope did not necessarily indicate an unchanging series: 180 of the 3,969 zero-slope areas had observed annual variation, including 78 with positive observations after 2021. A product audit revealed a synchronous pulse of first-positive annual observations in 2017 and increases in 2023 across inside and surrounding cohorts; processing effects remain unresolved. Among 4,253 areas with paired inside and 1 km surrounding trends, 347 had both slopes positive, 70 only the inside slope positive, and 249 only the surrounding slope positive. Among 150 positive-trend reserves with usable deeper-interior data, 42 retained a positive slope in the 1–5 km interior band. The assessment provides a national baseline for conservation screening that retains spatial eligibility, product non-detection and annual trajectories. Its transferable lesson is to use satellite trend categories to direct local investigation, with product-compatibility checks and annual observations to interpret changes that full-period slopes can miss.

## Keywords

artificial light at night; protected areas; VIIRS; remote sensing; conservation monitoring

## 1. Introduction

Protected-area managers need repeatable information about pressures that extend beyond reserve boundaries. Artificial light at night (ALAN) is one such pressure: lighting can alter behaviour, physiology and species interactions, and its influence is not confined to the land on which it originates [@longcore2004; @gaston2013; @hirt2023]. Satellite night-lights provide broad spatial coverage and a growing temporal record, making them attractive for screening reserve estates that cannot be surveyed comprehensively from the ground.

Previous studies have documented increasing lighting within and around protected areas [@gaston2015; @guette2018; @mu2021], and skyglow affecting conservation-priority areas [@garrett2020]. Translating those observations into a monitoring system requires decisions about measurement support, surrounding-land comparisons and the meaning of a trend. On the nominal 500 m product grid, some reserves contain too few pixels for a stable summary. In low-radiance landscapes, annual composites contain many zeros. A surrounding buffer may include ocean or neighbouring protected tenure, neither of which represents the adjacent unprotected landscape. These choices influence both the apparent environmental pattern and the set of reserves that an agency can assess.

Satellite upward radiance also differs from sky brightness and the illumination experienced by organisms. Spectral sensitivity, atmospheric propagation and changing lighting technologies separate these quantities [@falchi2016; @kyba2023; @levin2025; @bara2025]. A satellite-based dark-sky indicator therefore measures a particular aspect of reserve condition. It cannot certify ecological darkness. The monitoring task is to identify where the measured signal merits investigation and where the product lacks adequate support.

Temporal summaries introduce another distinction. A positive robust slope is useful for screening long-term change, but a zero slope can occur in a series containing recent or episodic positive values. Similarly, simultaneous positive slopes inside and outside a reserve suggest different follow-up questions from an increase confined to one zone, without revealing a lighting source. Retaining annual values alongside categories makes these distinctions accessible to managers.

We assessed the Australian protected-area estate using annual VIIRS-derived night-lights for 2013-2024. The primary questions were: which reserves have sufficient raster support; how do full-period inside trends compare with trends in adjacent unprotected land; and what information is lost when annual observations are reduced to slope signs? Interior bands and marginal temporal-resampling summaries supplied additional diagnostics. The main contribution is a worked national application of satellite screening for conservation, with eligibility and temporal interpretation carried through to reserve-level examples.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Protected-area estate and night-lights product

We used the 2024 Collaborative Australian Protected Areas Database (CAPAD), dissolving polygons by stable protected-area identifier (`PA_ID`) and repairing invalid geometries. The resulting 11,133 identifiers were the analytical units. Distance and area calculations used the Australian equal-area projection EPSG:3577. A fixed 2024 estate was assessed retrospectively across all years; the analysis does not compare contemporaneous annual reserve boundaries or estimate an effect of designation.

Annual inputs were the 2013-2024 VIIRS-derived Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs in the Hengl/OpenGeoHub night-lights dataset, version 0.4 [@hengl2025nightlights], based on the Annual VNL V2 methodology [@elvidge2021]. The derivative dataset rescales the source values and also contains earlier extrapolated years; we used only its 2013-2024 annual layers. Each GeoTIFF's scale and offset were applied, including the scale-metadata change in 2022-2024. Retained source tags identify the 2022 input as a V2.2 masked composite combining Suomi-NPP and NOAA-20, while 2023-2024 identify Suomi-NPP masked composites. The earlier archived processing script supports V2.1 masked inputs for 2013-2021, but does not fully reproduce the later deposit's encoding (Supporting Methods S2). Throughout, *product-scaled radiance* means these metadata-adjusted values. We report scaled product units and do not convert them to physical radiance, ground illuminance or sky brightness.

The smallest positive decoded pixel value in the Australian audit window was 10 product units in every year from 2013 to 2024. This observed floor concerns individual pixels; spatial means can be smaller. Integer quantisation and upstream masking both constrain low-radiance observations, with their separate contributions unresolved. Zeros were retained as product non-detections, without imputation or smoothing; negative source codes and no-data values were excluded. Missing measurements remained missing, and annual layers were neither interpolated nor substituted across years. Source versions and checksums were preserved. A targeted continuity audit compared stored pixel values, metadata-adjusted distributions and first-positive annual observations across this boundary.

### 2.2 Spatial summaries and eligibility

For each protected area and year, we calculated inside mean radiance, median, 95th percentile and the fraction of valid pixels greater than zero. Raster extraction used centre-based polygon selection (`all_touched=False`) and unweighted summaries of the selected valid cells. Mean radiance was the primary intensity summary. The other metrics described the distribution, particularly its zero mass, rather than providing alternative outcomes selected for stronger trends.

We constructed 1 and 5 km outward buffers and compared raw geometric buffers, land-masked buffers, and land buffers excluding overlap with all protected areas. The last definition, termed the *unprotected-land buffer*, was the primary surrounding comparison. Land masking used Australian Coastline 50K 2024 version 1-1 polygons [@hammerton2024coastline], derived from 2022–2024 Sentinel-2 imagery and approximating mean high water. Six small reserve polygons with no intersection with this land source were excluded from land-dependent surrounding comparisons; none belonged to the eligible inside cohort. Removing ocean and protected tenure defines the adjacent unprotected landscape more consistently; it does not locate the source of incoming light. The 1 km buffer supplied the main screening comparison and the 5 km buffer broader context. Buffers and inward bands retained the repaired CAPAD boundaries without simplification. Circular arcs used 128 segments per quadrant, giving a maximum chord error below 0.19 m at 10 km; a raster-support convergence check is described in Supporting Methods S3. Distances are projected EPSG:3577 distances, not exact geodesic distances. Comparisons near documented coastline-source exceptions were flagged as provisional in the reserve-level results and require local map validation before site use (Supporting Methods S3).

Every zone-year summary required at least 10 valid pixels at the product's nominal 500 m resolution. Values failing this threshold were missing. Inside trend estimation additionally required at least eight valid years; surrounding trends followed the same temporal rule. These thresholds favour reserves with adequate area and shape for raster extraction, so eligibility was reported separately for each analysis. Pixel counts indicate raster support, not independent observations of ecological exposure.

Interior bands extended 0-1, 1-5 and 5-10 km inward from reserve boundaries. We reported their individual feasibility and described 2024 patterns among areas meeting the pixel rule in all three bands. This complete-band subset was treated as a geometry-selected diagnostic. A separate sensitivity recalculated slopes for the 1–5 km band, using the same pixel and temporal rules. We compared whole-reserve and band categories only among areas eligible for both, holding the corrected 1 km surrounding slope fixed. A subsequent review-stage check added 0–1 km slopes and annual zero status for that same cohort. To assess the magnitude underlying area-weighted counts, we grouped the 420 positive whole-reserve slopes into terciles and summed polygon areas within each.

### 2.3 Full-period trends and screening categories

Theil-Sen slopes summarised 2013-2024 change in annual mean product-scaled radiance. For areas with eligible inside and 1 km unprotected-land trends, we defined four descriptive categories: both slopes positive (Q1), only the inside slope positive (Q2), neither slope positive (Q3), and only the surrounding slope positive (Q4). Non-positive means zero or negative; Q3 therefore includes dimming. The inside-minus-surrounding slope difference was retained as a descriptive contrast.

Categories used strict point-estimate signs, without a significance or minimum-effect threshold. They describe the direction of full-period median pairwise slopes, not the timing, cause or ecological importance of change. A non-positive surrounding slope, for example, does not imply that the final annual value is no higher than the first.

To distinguish zero slopes from unchanging observations, we examined annual series for every eligible area with an exactly zero inside slope. We counted series with any variation among valid annual means, series with all observed values zero, and constant non-zero series. A separate, explicitly defined late-positive pattern required all 12 annual values to be valid, values in 2013-2021 to be zero, and at least one positive value in 2022-2024. This was a descriptive check added during manuscript review; it did not redefine the trend categories or test for ecological onset. To examine the 2022 product boundary, we calculated annual first-positive fractions among complete series that were zero in every preceding year. Inside-reserve comparisons used fixed distance strata from positive 2013 raster-cell centres, avoiding inclusion of subsequent detections in the reference mask. These were compatibility diagnostics, not tests identifying the cause of change (Supporting Methods S2).

Four named areas were retained as illustrative annual trajectories: Neerabup (WA), Henderson (WA), Black Mountain (ACT) and Bungonia (NSW). Selection was illustrative rather than representative; Black Mountain deliberately shows that Q3 can contain declining radiance. We plotted all annual inside and surrounding values and reported their full-period slopes.

### 2.4 Temporal sensitivity

Archived per-area resampling summaries were retained as a temporal sensitivity diagnostic. Each selected area received independently generated length-three moving-block resamples of its annual year-value pairs; slopes were recomputed and pooled by area. We report marginal medians, 2.5th-97.5th percentiles and fractions of resampled slopes greater than zero. The original procedure selected dominant IBRA regions within 300 outer iterations, affecting how many resamples each area received. These marginal distributions provide neither joint spatial uncertainty nor calibrated confidence intervals. Supporting Methods S1 describes the algorithm and its interpretation.

### 2.5 Reproducibility

The Python pipeline uses versioned source identities, pinned dependencies, stable output keys and run manifests. After independent review identified coarse buffer geometry and an unsuitable land mask, all surrounding and interior-band observations were re-extracted for 2013–2024. Geometry-only and coastline changes were compared separately (Supporting Methods S3). Whole-reserve annual observations were retained with exact keyed equality checks, and their trend estimates were verified. The corrected analysis has its own input and output manifest; it does not overwrite the earlier archived baseline. Publicly archived processed outputs and the remaining code/data access boundaries are specified below.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Assessment feasibility and 2024 radiance

Of 11,133 protected areas, 4,426 (39.8%) met the inside pixel and temporal eligibility rules (Table 1; Fig. 1). Paired inside and 1 km unprotected-land trends were available for 4,253 areas; the corresponding 5 km comparison was available for 4,370. These are result-specific denominators, reflecting reserve geometry, surrounding-land masking and raster support. Compared with the earlier coarse-support implementation, 407 reserves gained paired eligibility and 12 lost it; 57 of the 3,846 reserves common to both versions changed sign category (Table S5).

Among the 4,426 eligible areas, 3,844 (86.9%) had zero inside mean radiance in 2024. The remaining 582 were spatially concentrated near developed coastal and urban regions (Fig. 1). Eligible identifiers contained 99.8% of summed polygon area. Reserves with positive whole-reserve slopes contained 37.6% of eligible summed area, compared with 9.5% by count (Table 1). The lowest slope tercile (140 reserves; slopes below 0.040 product units per year) contributed 99.1% of the positive class's summed area (Table S4). The other 280 reserves, with slopes above 0.04 product units per year, contained 0.32% of eligible summed area; this cutoff describes the observed tercile division, not an ecological-effect threshold. These area summaries weight each reserve by its full polygon area: overlapping designations may be counted more than once, and the area assigned to a trend class is not the area actually illuminated or changing.

Interior support decreased with distance from the boundary. In 2024, the 0-1 km band was valid for 4,426 areas, the 1-5 km band for 1,398, and all three bands for 369 (3.3% of the estate). Within the complete-band subset, the shares with any positive pixels were 17.62%, 8.94% and 3.52%, respectively (Fig. S1). Median fractions of positive pixels were zero in every band. The observed decline is conditional on this selected subset.

### 3.2 Full-period slopes and information concealed by zeros

Inside slopes were positive for 420 eligible areas (9.5%), negative for 37 (0.8%), and exactly zero for 3,969 (89.7%; Fig. 2). The mean across all eligible areas was 0.0283 scaled product units per year (SD 0.295). Among the 457 non-zero slopes, the median was 0.098 and the interquartile range 0.0075-0.4164.

All 3,969 zero-slope areas had complete 12-year series. Of these, 3,786 had zero annual means throughout, three had constant non-zero means, and 180 (4.5%) had observed annual variation. Seventy-eight (2.0% of zero-slope areas) met the late-positive definition: nine zero-valued years followed by at least one positive annual value during 2022-2024. Thus the zero-slope count exceeds the number of unchanging observed series and cannot be interpreted as an absence of measured change.

First-positive fractions rose synchronously in 2017 and 2023 across the inside and two surrounding cohorts (Fig. S3; Table S2). Inside first detections numbered 53 in 2017, 14 in 2022, 31 in 2023 and 33 in 2024; 64 of the 78 late-positive series began in 2023–2024. Across the common raster support, zero-to-positive transitions increased from 28,035 in 2022 to 57,328 in 2023, while positive-to-zero transitions decreased from 21,520 to 12,031. These calendar patterns leave processing effects unresolved despite the absence of a first-detection spike in 2022 itself.

Marginal temporal resampling produced narrow ranges for some selected slopes and wide ranges for others (Fig. S2; Table S1). These ranges describe sensitivity to the resampling procedure. They were not used to assign categories or to infer a reliable national ordering of reserves.

### 3.3 Inside and surrounding trends

Among the 4,253 paired areas, both slopes were positive in 347 (Q1; 8.2%), only the inside slope was positive in 70 (Q2; 1.6%), neither slope was positive in 3,587 (Q3; 84.3%), and only the surrounding slope was positive in 249 (Q4; 5.9%; Fig. 3). The 596 areas with positive surrounding slopes therefore included 249 that an inside-positive screen alone would omit.

The four illustrative trajectories show how these categories translate into different temporal patterns (Fig. 4). Neerabup had positive inside and surrounding slopes of 8.150 and 3.847 scaled product units per year. Its inside annual mean rose from 68.814 in 2013 to 190.339 in 2024. Bungonia had an inside mean of zero throughout, while its surrounding series varied and had a positive slope of 0.417.

Henderson had positive inside and surrounding slopes of 1.731 and 2.833. Its surrounding classification changed from non-positive to positive when spatial support was corrected, illustrating the sensitivity of a coastal site's interpretation to land masking. At Black Mountain, inside and surrounding slopes were both negative (-1.037 and -3.952). Its Q3 classification denotes two non-positive trends, not a stable or unlit site.

The 1–5 km interior-band sensitivity was feasible for 1,341 of the 4,253 paired reserves (31.5%). Within this matched subset, 27 of 112 Q1 reserves retained a positive interior-band slope; the other 85 became Q4 when the band replaced the whole-reserve mean. Fifteen of 38 Q2 reserves retained a positive band slope and 23 became Q3. All 85 Q1 losses had exactly zero 1–5 km slopes, but only 73 had all-zero band histories; the other 12 contained positive annual observations. The 1,103 original Q3 and 88 Q4 reserves retained their categories (Table S3). Thus 42 of 150 positive whole-reserve trends extended to a positive trend in this deeper band. All 1,341 matched reserves also supplied 0–1 km slopes. Of the 150 positive whole-reserve cases, 100 had positive 0–1 km slopes and zero 1–5 km slopes, while 38 had positive slopes in both bands (Table S4). The pattern also occurred for measured dimming: 11 of 12 matched reserves with negative whole-reserve slopes had negative 0–1 km slopes. Nine of those 11 had zero 1–5 km slopes and two had negative slopes; the twelfth reserve had zero slopes in both bands.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 What the satellite screen contributes to conservation monitoring

This assessment identifies where annual night-lights can support reserve-level screening and makes the exclusions visible. Its practical contribution is the combination of a fixed protected-area estate, explicit raster-support rules, corrected surrounding-land comparisons and inspectable annual trajectories. Together these provide a baseline that agencies can revisit as compatible observations become available.

The paired comparison adds information beyond an inside-only trend map. The 249 areas with positive surrounding but non-positive inside slopes warrant examination of change in the adjacent unprotected landscape, even though their inside trend category would not flag brightening. Areas with both slopes positive raise questions about conditions within the reserve as well as its surroundings. Correcting buffers for ocean and protected tenure makes these comparisons more consistent across coastal reserves and neighbouring protected areas.

These observations complement earlier protected-area lighting assessments [@gaston2015; @guette2018; @garrett2020; @mu2021]. The transferable element is the monitoring design: define the estate and spatial support, inspect product non-detection, distinguish inside from surrounding change, and retain the observations underlying each trend. The Australian results also show why this sequence needs more than a single national brightening percentage.

### 4.2 A zero slope is an incomplete monitoring result

The distinction between a zero annual value and a zero temporal slope is consequential. An annual zero indicates non-detection in the selected product. A zero Theil-Sen slope is the median of pairwise rates of change and can arise despite positive annual observations. For example, nine zero values in a 12-year series generate 36 zero pairwise slopes out of 66, enough to yield a zero median even if all three later values increase.

This was not merely a hypothetical limitation: 180 zero-slope areas had varying annual means, and 78 showed the specified late-positive pattern. Their existing categories need not be discarded, because those categories answer a full-period trend question. Monitoring late or intermittent product detections requires reading the annual series or adding a separately defined temporal flag. The synchronous 2017 pulse and 2023 increases caution against interpreting these flags as environmental onset. The mixed-sensor 2022 composite could have suppressed some faint detections before the 2023 increase; this is a possible processing explanation, not an established cause. Persistence into subsequent years does not distinguish processing from environmental change. The 78 cases therefore identify first-positive product observations for investigation, not confirmed arrivals of lighting. A trend screen and a recent-change alert should not be treated as interchangeable products.

Black Mountain demonstrates that the large Q3 category contains dimming as well as zeros. Retaining annual observations helps distinguish those trajectories and inspect fluctuations concealed by a single slope sign. These examples favour plain category names and ready access to the underlying series.

### 4.3 From a national flag to local investigation

A useful agency workflow begins by identifying whether a reserve and its surroundings are evaluable. For an eligible site, interpretation of a temporal flag should start by checking for product-version or processing boundaries. The next steps are to inspect the annual values and sign category, consider magnitude and temporal sensitivity, and examine local conservation context. At Neerabup, the paired increase directs attention to both spatial zones. At Bungonia, the first follow-up concerns the surrounding landscape and whether changes below satellite detection are occurring inside. These are investigation pathways, not claims about the sources of light at either reserve.

Ground-based sky-brightness measurements, lighting inventories and information on facilities, roads, development and terrain can distinguish explanations that national radiance summaries cannot. Site priority would additionally depend on ecological sensitivity, conservation objectives and practical scope for intervention. The magnitude of a satellite slope alone cannot supply that priority. The percentile ranges in Table S1 help readers inspect sensitivity of selected estimates, but are not formal confidence statements or a basis for competitive ranking.

Product zeros also deserve continued stewardship. Non-detection across a large reserve may coexist with ecologically relevant illumination at a small facility or boundary. Conversely, satellite brightening does not demonstrate a harmful biological response. Opportunistic occurrence records cannot resolve that additional inference without accounting for sampling processes that can confound ecological interpretation [@phillips2009; @fithian2015; @baker2024]. We recommend standardised repeated surveys with suitable detection, effort and habitat information to investigate biological consequences.

### 4.4 Limits and transfer to future assessments

The study's scope is constrained first by its measurement product. VIIRS-derived upward radiance does not measure ground illumination, spectral composition or sky brightness, and changing lighting technologies can alter relationships among these quantities [@falchi2016; @kyba2023; @levin2025; @bara2025]. The 10-unit observed positive pixel floor shows that low-end values are discretised. Zeros may reflect both upstream masking and derivative encoding, whose separate contributions cannot be recovered from the deposited workflow; the 86.9% zero share is not a calibrated measure of ecological darkness. Applying metadata reconciles the observed integer encodings but does not eliminate upstream processing, atmospheric or spectral influences. A switch to white LEDs can reduce DNB radiance without reducing visible illumination because the sensor is insensitive below approximately 500 nm [@kyba2017]. Black Mountain's declining product radiance therefore does not establish that the reserve became darker, nor does it identify an LED conversion. Spatial mixing is also relevant: resampling an approximately 750 m DNB footprint onto a nominal 500 m grid does not resolve lighting sources at reserve boundaries. Neither robust slopes nor temporal resampling recovers changes below detection.

Second, eligibility is selective: 60.2% of protected-area identifiers lacked sufficient support for the main assessment. National frequency statements concern the eligible subset, with small and narrow reserves under-represented. Although eligible reserves contain nearly all summed polygon area, this does not remove the monitoring gap for numerous small reserves. The 369 complete-band areas likewise cannot establish a national boundary-to-interior effect. In the separate matched-band sensitivity, most positive whole-reserve slopes did not remain positive 1–5 km inward. The 100 cases with positive 0–1 km but zero 1–5 km slopes support spatial concentration of the measured trend near the boundary within this cohort. Pixel mixing, product non-detection and change in other parts of a reserve still limit interpretation. The sensitivity neither identifies spillover nor replaces the national screen. Counts of protected-area identifiers should not be read as area-weighted exposure or statistically independent ecological replicates. The coastline source also has documented bridge and estuary errors; affected surrounding comparisons require local map checks (Supporting Methods S3).

Third, the sign categories are descriptive. Small positive estimates qualify alongside larger ones: the positive class's large summed area is overwhelmingly carried by its lowest slope tercile. This prevents interpreting the 37.6% area share as widespread change of substantial magnitude. The marginal resampling ranges do not propagate measurement error, boundary uncertainty or spatial covariance. Formal statements about joint category confidence or reserve ordering would require a different inferential analysis.

Finally, the fixed CAPAD 2024 estate means that historical observations refer to land protected in that snapshot, including any land designated later in the study period. Future updates should record both boundary and night-lights product identities and separate changes in the monitoring estate from environmental change. With these conditions explicit, annual satellite observations can support repeatable conservation screening: they identify useful places to investigate while retaining the temporal and spatial information needed to interpret each flag.
